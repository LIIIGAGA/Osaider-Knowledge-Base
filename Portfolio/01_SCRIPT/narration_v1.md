[VISUAL: 项目总览图]

> Osaider is a narrative-driven exploration game. You uncover its story through the spaces you move through, and the choices you make inside them. You interact and converse with a palace that endlessly retells its own history — piecing it back together, module by module, uncovering the secrets and truths buried inside its structure.

*（这段是你0716Review标题页的官方项目介绍，比docx那版更精炼，我放在最前面当开场定义。）*

[VISUAL: 红蓝双色系统第一次亮相]

> It takes place at sea, cut off from the outside world — a place with no past and no history, home to people forgotten by god. Every citizen's life follows the same cycle: weapons built by day, melted down and returned to raw material by night, put back into production the next morning. A weapons factory fighting an enemy that may only be imagined — an enemy called Osaider. The cycle doesn't exist for the sake of war. It exists to sustain the appearance of deterrence — an absurd, self-perpetuating ritual.

[VISUAL: Archivist / Whalemen 立绘对照]

> Two factions have formed around this cycle. The Archivists — the official realm — don't merely preserve memory. They decide what's worthy of being remembered, shaping what people think, quietly. The Whalemen are the shadow society underneath them: workers who came to realise the war stays distant and unseen, that production is meaningless, that the official history doesn't add up — and who are now secretly planning to steal materials, resources, and energy from the factory, to break the cycle and escape.

[VISUAL: 字卡]

> The two factions are locked in a struggle over whether to break this loop, or preserve it. And that struggle is really a question for you: whether truth and meaning exist at all. If a lie is there to protect us — are you willing to serve it?
>
> Your task is to piece together the whole palace, break the cycle, and find a path toward the future.

*（这几句是你0716Review "Story synopsis & World setting" 那页原话，问题意识比我原来写的更锋利，我基本照搬，只做了口语化的小调整。）*

[VISUAL: 字幕式提纲：三个关键词逐一打出]

> Three ideas run through the project.
>
> **Constructed history** — every space you build carries a fragment of memory. Rebuilding the palace, module by module, is also an act of reconstructing its history. The palace is never finished, the way history itself is never finished. Whether it's personal memory, held by the Whalemen, or the official record, held by the Archivists — each is only ever one side. We never get the whole picture.
>
> **Bureaucracy and endless production** — weapons built every day, destroyed every night, materials recycled and fed back in. The system exists only to sustain itself. The individual disappears entirely inside this machine; these vast structures are the state's will made visible.
>
> **Environmental discipline versus the player's will** — to progress at all, you have to follow the system's rules: the same item checks, the same spatial loop, over and over. The environment gradually trains you into compliance. An unfinished palace feels like an unsolved mystery, pulling you back again and again. But at what point do you grow tired, see through it, and choose to walk away from a ritual that never ends?

> Osaider's connection to this year's theme runs on two levels. At the level of story, this is a society that has adapted to a permanent state of war — whether or not that war was ever real. At the level of form, I've taken real institutional structures — the archive, the bureaucratic document, oral history — and adapted them into playable space and mechanics. And underneath both, the player adapts too: progress requires compliance with the system's rules, repeated until it becomes habit. The game trains you the way the palace trains its own citizens.

## 2. Research and References — Term2 Portfolio已讲过，简略带过

[VISUAL: 论文标题页 / Spatial Storylet网站截图]

> Osaider isn't just informed by my dissertation research — it's the practice-based half of it. My argument there is that most so-called non-linear narrative games are only non-linear on a timeline; real spatial non-linearity means treating space itself as narrative grammar. Osaider is the design built to test that, through three layers working together: a fixed text fragment in each module — the lexical layer; the spatial form that fragment sits inside, which changes how you read it — the contextual layer; and the rules connecting modules to each other — the grammatical layer, tested here through the dice check that decides what gets revealed next. The palace doesn't pre-exist. It's produced by the act of walking through it.

*（这段是我读了你的论文《Spatial Storylets: Extending Ergodic Narrative into Three-Dimensional Space》第4章"Design Practice"之后写的——你论文里明确点名OSAIDER是这套理论的practice-based实践案例，"lexical/contextual/grammatical"三层模型直接对应你的Figma文字版原型(lexical)+UE空间模块(contextual)+骰子检定驱动的模块生成系统(grammatical)。"The palace does not pre-exist; it is produced by the act of traversal"这句是你论文原话，我保留了这个意象。这已经不是"锦上添花"了——这是整个设计方法论的来源，比Foucault/Orwell这些主题性参照分量重得多，所以放在了Research板块最前面，其他参照往后排。）*

[VISUAL: 苏维埃宫模型/明信片/建筑渲染图]

> My research starts from a historical structure: the unbuilt Palace of the Soviets. Planned for the site of Moscow's demolished Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, the project was never finished — the site became, at one point, the world's largest swimming pool — and the cathedral was eventually rebuilt in its place. History looked like it had come full circle. But the Palace itself never disappeared: it kept reappearing in film, art, stamps, packaging — more alive as an imagined symbol than it ever was as a building. That's the question I kept coming back to: if a structure is endlessly demolished and rebuilt, how do people live inside that cycle?

[VISUAL: The Tartar Steppe / Il Deserto dei Tartari (1976) 剧照]

> The second reference is literary — Dino Buzzati's *The Tartar Steppe*. A soldier is posted to a remote fortress to guard against an enemy that never comes, for thirty years. But that never-arriving war is what gives his life meaning. The fortress isn't protecting anyone from an army. It's protecting the meaning people have built their lives around.
>
> Both of these sit inside a wider frame — Camus, and the idea that we're born to resist absurdity, that you have to imagine Sisyphus happy, pushing the same boulder up the same hill, forever.

[VISUAL: 加缪引语字卡 / Sisyphus图]

> Osaider takes this same structure — the endless cycle, the enemy that may not exist, the fortress that protects belief rather than bodies — and makes it playable. The difference is that in Osaider, you're not Drogo, waiting passively for thirty years. The cycle responds to you. Whether you keep feeding it, or find a way to walk out, is a choice the game actually gives you.

[VISUAL: 参考画面快切，2-3秒一张]

> A few other threads run alongside this. George Orwell's *1984* — the idea that a war might be manufactured, a Ministry of Truth rewriting the past — sits directly behind Osaider's central mystery. On the systems side, the ideology-driven, item-based narrative owes something to games like *Papers, Please*, *Disco Elysium*, and *Return of Obra Dinn* — object- and document-driven storytelling, mechanics that track ideological position numerically instead of branching outright. And Michel Foucault's writing on the archive, and the relationship between power and knowledge, sits behind the Archivists' core belief: that truth exists only through documentation.

## 3. Development — 4:30，全片核心（这节内容不变，未修改）

[VISUAL: 关卡演化对比表做成动态时间轴]

> Osaider went through two complete rebuilds before it became what it is now. I want to show you both of them, because the dead ends taught me as much as the parts that worked.

**3.1 关卡与系统演化**

> It started as a small interior space with a top-down character you could walk around and interact with directly. That was Prototype 01 — it proved the basic loop of zooming in, picking things up, opening doors.
>
> Prototype 02 pushed further: I built a full storylet narrative system, five separate story modules chained together. And then I abandoned the whole thing.

**3.2 第一次转折**

> The problem was the character itself. Walking a body through space didn't match what the game was actually about — paging through an archive, not exploring on foot. So Prototype 03 dropped the character entirely. Mouse-driven interaction, camera actor with spline paths for cinematic movement. The scale of the space jumped too — from a small room to something much larger, much more like an institution.

**3.3 第二次转折：Storylet系统的放弃**

> The Storylet system from Prototype 02 — a whole subsystem of base classes and module assemblers — didn't survive the rebuild. It was replaced with something simpler: every module already exists in the level from the start, hidden and collision-disabled, and gets revealed when you need it. Better performance, easier to debug, more predictable. That's not a compromise — it's a decision I'd make again.

**3.4 核心循环的搭建**

> The loop that survived all of this: explore, interact, collect three items, submit them, roll — two dice plus your accumulated ideology value, against a threshold of seven — and the result decides which module reveals itself next.
>
> [VISUAL: BP_CoreDevice简化流程动画]
>
> One smaller decision worth mentioning: the drag-and-interact system originally shared a single parent class across every module, which made it fragile. Switching to a Blueprint Interface instead gave the design a lot more flexibility.

**3.5 UI迭代**

> The interface went through the same process. The submission panel alone has three distinct versions — centred composition, then strict symmetry — each one solving a specific problem with the last. The intro sequence has ten documented versions, moving from a static screen to a fully animated video background.
>
