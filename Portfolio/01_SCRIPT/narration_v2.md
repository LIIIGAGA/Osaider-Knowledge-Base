## 1. Intro — 目标1:30，⚠现在念出来大概2:20-2:30，需要精简

[VISUAL: 项目总览图]

> Osaider is a narrative-driven exploration game where players uncover stories through the spaces they traverse and the choices they make.

> Players interact and converse with a palace that endlessly retells its own history, uncovering hidden secrets and truths embedded within its structures.

> Your task is to piece together the whole palace, break the cycle and discover a path toward the future.

[VISUAL: 红蓝双色系统第一次亮相]

> The nation is built around the fear of a mysterious enemy: Osaider. But as players traverse the palace and piece together its fragmented history, a deeper question emerges — what if Osaider does not exist at all?

> Life within the palace follows a single cycle: by day they manufacture weapons, by night those weapons are melted down and reborn as raw materials, ready to be produced again the following day. The cycle exists not for war, but as a performative ritual of deterrence.

[VISUAL: Archivist / Whalemen 立绘对照]

> Two factions shape the world in secret: the Archivists control official memory, determining what is worthy of being remembered; the Whalemen are an underground society who have seen through the myth, quietly planning their escape. Players navigate between these forces through a Dice Check system — every collectible item carries a hidden Ideology value (IDEO) that shapes narrative outcomes and unlocks faction-specific storylets.

> How to break the cycle, find a path to the future, and retell this buried tale anew, how to give meaning to the loop itself — all of that is yours to decide.

[VISUAL: 字幕式提纲：三个关键词逐一打出]

> Three ideas run through the project.

> **Constructed History** — Each spatial module carries a fragment of historical memory. As players repeatedly rebuild the palace, they are also reconstructing its history. Yet the palace can never truly be completed—just as history itself can never be fully known. Whether through **personal memory (Whalemen)** or **official records (Archivist)**, each offers only one perspective on the past; the whole picture always remains beyond our reach.

> **Bureaucracy & Endless Production** — Weapons are produced every day, destroyed every night, and their materials recycled back into production. The system exists for no purpose other than to sustain itself. Within this vast machinery of the state, the individual disappears entirely, while the monumental structures become physical manifestations of the state’s will.

> **Environmental Discipline vs. Player Agency** — To progress, players must obey the system’s rules of interaction. Through the endless repetition of item checks and spatial interactions, the environment gradually “disciplines” the player into adaptive patterns of behaviour. The unfinished palace appears to conceal an unresolved mystery, enticing the player to return again and again. But at what point will you grow tired of the repetition, recognise the nature of the system, and choose to walk away from this endless ritual?

> Osaider's connection to this year's theme **“Adaptation”** runs on two levels. At the level of **story**, this is a society that has adapted to a permanent state of war — whether or not that war was ever real. At the level of  **spatial form,** I've taken real institutional structures — the archive, the bureaucratic document, oral history — and adapted them into playable space and mechanics. And underneath both, the player adapts too: progress requires compliance with the system's rules, repeated until it becomes habit. The game trains you the way the palace trains its own citizens.

## 2. Research and References

[VISUAL: 论文标题页 / Spatial Storylet网站截图]

> Osaider isn't just informed by my dissertation research — it's the practice-based half of it. My argument there is that most so-called non-linear narrative games are only non-linear on a timeline; real spatial non-linearity means treating space itself as narrative grammar. Osaider is the design built to test that, through three layers working together: a fixed text fragment in each module — the lexical layer; the spatial form that fragment sits inside, which changes how you read it — the contextual layer; and the rules connecting modules to each other — the grammatical layer, tested here through the dice check that decides what gets revealed next. The palace doesn't pre-exist. It's produced by the act of walking through it.

[VISUAL: 苏维埃宫模型/明信片/建筑渲染图]

> My research starts from a historical event: the unbuilt Palace of the Soviets. Planned for the site of Moscow's demolished Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, the project was never finished — the site became, at one point, the world's largest swimming pool — and the cathedral was eventually rebuilt in its place. However, the idea of building a grand palace is even more powerful than a real building. Even today, it continues to appear in films, artworks, stamps, and even product packaging.

[VISUAL: The Tartar Steppe / Il Deserto dei Tartari (1976) 剧照]

> The narrative design took inspiration from the novel The Tartar Steppe. A soldier is posted to a remote fortress to guard against an enemy that never comes for thirty years. But that never-arriving war is what gives his life meaning. The fortress isn't protecting anyone from an army. It's protecting the meaning people have built their lives around.

> Both of these sit within a wider frame — Camus and the idea of confronting the absurd: that we have to imagine Sisyphus happy, pushing the same boulder up the same hill forever.

[VISUAL: 加缪引语字卡 / Sisyphus图]

> Osaider takes this same structure — the endless cycle, the enemy that may not exist, the fortress that protects belief rather than bodies — and makes it playable.

[VISUAL:Osaider游戏录屏的循环结尾CyclesReflection部分画面]

> The difference is that in Osaider, you're not Drogo, waiting passively for thirty years. Every 10 minutes, you have a chance to choose, whether you want to return to the cycle or exit to break it.

[VISUAL: 参考画面快切，2-3秒一张]

> In addition, George Orwell’s 1984, the film Metropolis, Michel Foucault’s theories of the archive and power/knowledge, and games such as Papers, Please and Disco Elysium have all influenced my narrative and game mechanics to varying degrees. Meanwhile, the architectural and visual design draws inspiration from Soviet Constructivism, Soviet Modernism, Brutalism, and Monumentalism.

## 3. Development — 4:30，全片核心（内容未修改）

[VISUAL: 关卡演化对比表做成动态时间轴]

> Osaider went through two rebuilds before it became what it is now.

### 3.1 关卡与系统演化

> It started as a small interior space with a top-down character you could walk around and interact with directly. I experimented with the basic loop of zooming in, revealing environment, triggering dice check and opening doors.

> Building on this, I developed a more refined environment. However, I soon realised that these fragmented scenes made it difficult to form a coherent image of the tower. At the same time, having the player character move through such an oversized space significantly weakened the narrative experience and limited the potential for more experimental spatial design.

> I therefore changed my interaction strategy, replacing character-based interaction with a UI-based system. This shifted the player’s attention away from a 3D avatar and towards the architecture itself, as well as the core mechanic of generating spatial modules through dice checks.

### 3.2 核心循环的搭建

> The final gameplay loop was established around six key stages: UI interaction, spatial storylet interaction, item collection, item submission, triggering a dice check, and finally the revealing the next module. The result of the dice check determines whether an Archivist or Whalemen module is revealed, before the cycle begins again.

## 4. Detailed Description of the Final Artefact — 2:50（按0716Review截图重写）

### 两个阵营的模块设计&塔的完全体造型展示

[VISUAL: 单模块的旋转展示&整体塔的展示视频clip]

> Two factions sit on either side of this system. The Archivists — red, official, ceremonial — don't merely preserve memory, they decide what's worthy of being remembered. The Whalemen — blue, underground — see through the myth, and are quietly preparing to steal what they need to break the cycle and escape.

> The complete version of the tower consists of 37 spatial modules, with over 70 built-in spatial interactions and approximately 4,500 words of narrative content.

### 塔的各种不完全体造型展示

> With each playthrough, the player may use different combinations of items, while the uncertainty introduced by the **dice check** further shapes the outcome. As a result, the tower can take on a wide variety of forms across different playthroughs.

[VISUAL: 循环结束的CyclesReflection界面展示]

> The game is structured around a 10-minute time loop. At the end of each loop, the player encounters the same series of reflective questions. Perhaps, after each journey through the palace, the player comes away with a different understanding of its world, and therefore gives different answers to the same questions.

> Ultimately, the player can choose either to return to the loop again or leave the game entirely — in other words, to break the cycle.

### 道具设计

[VISUAL: 道具关系图/几个道具展示]

> Every collectible item carries a hidden ideology value — Archivist-aligned or Whalemen-aligned. You can infer its ideological alignment through its colour, but its exact value is never explicitly revealed. **White items are neutral**, meaning that when they enter the dice check, the generation of the next spatial module is determined entirely by chance.

> The item icons use a **line-art style** to visually distinguish them from the architectural elements, while also maintaining consistency with the overall **UI visual language**. Beyond influencing the outcome of the dice check, these items also help strengthen narrative immersion and add finer-grained details to the world-building.

### 结尾

> Going forward, I want to keep exploring architecture and spatial interaction as a narrative medium — and gamification as a method of speculative and critical design.

> I hope you enjoy my game!
